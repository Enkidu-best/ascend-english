Условные, wish и косвенная речь Conditionals, wish & reported speech

Условные - 5 типов

ТемаФормулаПример
Тип 0 · фактIf + наст. → наст.If you heat ice, it melts.
Тип 1 · реальное будущееIf + наст. → will + гл.If it rains, I'll stay home.
Тип 2 · фантазия сейчасIf + прош. → would + гл.If I had time, I would help.
Тип 3 · фантазия прошлоеIf + had+V3 → would have+V3If I had known, I would have come.
Mixed · прошлое → сейчасIf + had+V3 → would + гл. (без have)If I had learned it earlier, I would work abroad now.

Косвенная речь

ТемаФормулаПример
Сдвиг времени назадsaid/asked → время на шаг назад"I work" → He said he worked.
Косвенный вопрособычный порядок, без do, время назад"Where do you work?" → She asked where I worked.
Yes/no вопрос+ if / whether"Do you need help?" → She asked if I needed help.
Команда / просьбаtold/asked + кого + to + inf"Send it." → He told me to send it.

wish - три модели

ТемаФормулаПример
wish + pastсожаление про сейчасI wish I knew the answer.
wish + had+V3сожаление про прошлоеI wish I hadn't sent it.
wish + wouldраздражающая привычкаI wish he would stop.

📖 Подробный разбор (если тема новая)

Главная идея условных

Условное предложение связывает две части: «если Х, то Y». Английский временами показывает, насколько ситуация реальна - и это ключ ко всему.

Прошедшее время после if в фантазиях (тип 2 и 3) - это НЕ «в прошлом», а сигнал «это не по-настоящему, воображаемое». Самый неинтуитивный момент: прими прошедшее время как маркер нереальности.

2-й тип подробно (фантазия про настоящее)

Схема: If + Past Simple → would + инфинитив. Смысл: сейчас всё иначе, я просто представляю. «If I had time, I would help» = времени нет, но я фантазирую.

Глагол «быть» в фантазиях для всех лиц часто ставят в форму were (I were, he were) - это subjunctive. Фраза «If I were you…» застывшая: всегда were, никогда was. Это стандартный способ дать совет.

3-й тип и mixed - в чём разница

3-й тип - фантазия про прошлое, которого не было: If + had+V3 → would have + V3. «If I had known, I would have come» (но не знал → не пришёл).

Mixed - прошлое условие, но результат в НАСТОЯЩЕМ: If + had+V3 → would + инфинитив (без have). «If I had learned English earlier, I would work abroad now». Отличие от 3-го только в том, куда смотрит результат: в прошлое (would have) или в сейчас (would).

Косвенная речь: сдвиг времени (backshift)

Когда пересказываешь чужие слова прошедшим (he said, she asked), время внутри «отступает на шаг назад»: present → past, present perfect / past → past perfect, will → would, can → could.

Пример: «We have signed» → They said they had signed.

Косвенные вопросы (частая ошибка)

Три изменения сразу: (1) обычный порядок слов - сначала подлежащее, потом глагол; (2) убираем do/does/did; (3) время сдвигается назад.

«Where do you work?» → She asked where I worked. Yes/no-вопрос → добавляем if или whether: «Do you need help?» → She asked if I needed help.

wish - три разные модели

wish + past simple = сожаление про сейчас (I wish I knew). wish + had+V3 = сожаление про прошлое (I wish I hadn’t sent it). wish + would = раздражающая чужая привычка, хочу, чтобы изменилась (I wish he would stop).

После wish не бывает will/won’t - только would.

Модальные глаголы Modal verbs

Возможность и вероятность

ТемаФормулаПример
canобщая возможность / свойствоWinters can be cold.
may / mightвозможно в конкретном случаеIt may rain later.
couldвежливая возможностьThis could work.

Догадка о настоящем

ТемаФормулаПример
mustуверен, что ДАHe must be tired.
can'tуверен, что НЕТShe can't be serious.
might / mayвозможноThey might be at lunch.

Догадка о прошлом (modal + have + V3)

ТемаФормулаПример
must have + V3наверняка былоHe must have left.
can't have + V3точно НЕ былоShe can't have known.
could/might have + V3могло / возможно былоI could have missed it.

Обязанность и совет

ТемаФормулаПример
must / have toнадо, обязанYou must sign here.
should / ought toстоит (совет)You should call them.
should have + V3надо было (упрёк)You should have told me.

📖 Подробный разбор (если тема новая)

can против may/might

can + be = общее свойство, «вообще бывает»: Winters can be cold (зимы в принципе бывают холодными). Маркер - sometimes, in general.

may / might = возможность в конкретный момент: It may rain later (возможно, именно сегодня).

Догадка: must / can’t / might

Когда делаешь вывод о настоящем: must = уверен, что ДА (He must be tired); can’t = уверен, что НЕТ, невозможно (She can’t be serious); might/may = возможно, не уверен.

Важно: отрицание уверенности - это can’t, а не mustn’t. mustn’t = запрет, а не «не может быть».

Догадка о прошлом: modal + have + V3

Та же логика, но про прошлое, всегда со связкой have + V3: must have done (наверняка сделал), can’t have done (точно не делал), could/might have done (мог / возможно сделал), should have done (надо было, но не сделал - упрёк).

Частая ошибка: терять have. Нужно «must have read», а не «must read», когда речь о прошлом выводе.

📖 Подробный разбор (если тема новая)

Предлоги at / in / on - как не путать

at - «точка»: конкретное время (at 6) и конкретное место-точка (at the station, at work).

in - «внутри»: месяцы, годы, части дня (in May, in 2026, in the morning). on - дни и даты (on Monday, on 5 May).

Артикли a / an - решает ЗВУК, а не буква

an ставим перед гласным ЗВУКОМ: an apple, an hour (h немая), an honest deal, an MBA (звучит «эм»).

a - перед согласным звуком: a university («ю»), a European, a one-time offer («ван»).

Фразовые глаголы учим как единицы

Значение фразового глагола часто не выводится из частей: put off = отложить, carry out = провести, follow up = связаться повторно, take on = взять на себя. Учи связку целиком + перевод.

unless / in case / late / lately

unless = если НЕ (Unless you hurry…). in case = заранее, на всякий случай (Take an umbrella in case it rains).

late = поздно (arrived late); lately = в последнее время (Have you… lately?). Разные слова, как hard/hardly.

get + причастие и главная ловушка

В разговорной речи get + V3 значит «стать / случилось само»: get broken (сломаться), get lost (потеряться), get married (пожениться).

Ключевая ошибка - время: в прошлом нужен GOT, не get (We got stuck, My phone got wet). И неправильные причастия: steal → stolen, tear → torn, break → broken.

Present perfect vs Past simple

have + V3 = опыт или незаконченное время (ever, so far, this week): I have been to three interviews this month.

Past simple = конкретный законченный момент (yesterday, in 2020, an hour ago): I got the job in 2023. Если есть точка во времени в прошлом - это past simple.

Past perfect и present perfect continuous

Past perfect (had + V3) = действие раньше другого момента в прошлом: By the time we called, they had closed the deal. Триггеры: by the time, before, already.

Present perfect continuous (have been + -ing) = действие началось и длится, важна длительность: I have been working on this all morning.

Why Leaves Change Color in Autumn

1 For most of the year, the leaves of deciduous trees appear green. This color comes from chlorophyll, the pigment that captures sunlight and drives photosynthesis, the process by which a plant makes its food. Chlorophyll is abundant during the growing season, and its strong green tone masks other pigments that are also present in the leaf.

2 Two of these hidden pigments are carotenoids, which produce yellow and orange tones, and they are present in the leaf throughout the summer. Because chlorophyll dominates, however, these colors remain invisible. As autumn approaches and days grow shorter, trees prepare for winter by shutting down food production. Chlorophyll breaks down and is not replaced, and as it disappears the yellows and oranges of the carotenoids are finally revealed.

3 Red and purple colors have a different origin. Unlike carotenoids, the pigments responsible for them, called anthocyanins, are not present all summer; they are manufactured in the leaf only in late season, when sugars become trapped there. Bright, cool, sunny days followed by cold nights favor their production. This is why the intensity of red foliage varies sharply from one year to the next, while yellow tends to appear more reliably.

4 Eventually a special layer of cells forms at the base of each leaf stalk, gradually severing the leaf from the tree. Once this layer is complete, the leaf falls. The tree, now dormant, will conserve its resources until spring, when new leaves and fresh chlorophyll begin the cycle again.

The Rise of Coffeehouses in 17th-Century Europe

1 When coffee arrived in the cities of seventeenth-century Europe, it was more than a novel drink; it reshaped how people met and exchanged ideas. The establishments that served it, known as coffeehouses, spread rapidly through London, Paris, and Vienna. Unlike taverns, where alcohol dulled the mind, coffeehouses offered a beverage that kept patrons alert, and this difference gave them a distinctive character.

2 For a modest entrance fee, often a single penny in London, a visitor could sit for hours, read the newspapers provided, and join any conversation at the long communal tables. Because the price of admission was low, men of different ranks mingled far more freely than they could elsewhere. Merchants, writers, and scientists debated side by side, and for this reason some coffeehouses earned the nickname "penny universities."

3 The exchange of information was not merely social. Certain houses became associated with particular trades. One London coffeehouse frequented by ship owners and insurers grew, over time, into a famous insurance market. Others served as informal stock exchanges or as places where the latest scientific findings were announced and disputed before they appeared in print.

4 Authorities did not always welcome these gatherings. Rulers occasionally viewed the free flow of talk with suspicion, fearing that criticism of the government might spread from the tables into the streets. Attempts to close the coffeehouses, however, met such resistance that they were quickly abandoned. The institution had become too useful, and too popular, to suppress.

The Mechanical Clock and the Measurement of Time

1 For most of human history, people measured time by watching the natural world. A sundial tracked the moving shadow cast by the sun, while a water clock marked the hours by the steady dripping of water from one container into another. Both methods had serious drawbacks. A sundial was useless at night or under a cloudy sky, and the flow of a water clock could speed up or slow down as the temperature changed.

2 In the towns of medieval Europe, a new device appeared that did not depend on the sun or the weather. The mechanical clock was driven by a slowly falling weight, and its motion was regulated by an escapement, a mechanism that released the turning gears in small, steady steps and produced the familiar ticking sound. The earliest of these clocks had no dial and no hands at all; instead, they rang a bell to announce each hour. In fact, the word "clock" comes from an old word for bell.

3 Such clocks were mounted in the towers of churches and town halls, where everyone could hear them. Their effect on daily life was profound. Work, markets, and prayer could now be scheduled against a single public time that the whole community shared, rather than against the changing position of the sun. For the first time, a town kept one official hour.

4 Early mechanical clocks were far from precise; they might gain or lose many minutes in a single day. Yet their reliability in any weather, and their public visibility, made them valuable despite this. Later inventions such as the pendulum would greatly improve accuracy, but by then the deeper change had already taken hold: people had begun to organize their lives around measured, uniform hours.
